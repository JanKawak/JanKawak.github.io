Multi-modal cancer classification pipeline

In a team of three, I built a large part of this pipeline. It predicts a patient's cancer type from two sources at once: the histopathology slide and the written pathology report. A slide foundation model (TITAN) turns each slide into a 768-dimensional embedding; a language model running on vLLM first cleans the report, then encodes it into 1024 dimensions. Both are fused and classified across 32 TCGA cancer types.

Most of our work went into making it trustworthy: a patient-level split so no patient leaks between train and test, one YAML config, fixed seeds, linting and tests in CI, and a hyperparameter sweep run as SLURM array jobs on the Snellius supercomputer.

32cancer types
2inputs: slide + report

How it works

1 Split by patient

Every patient lands in exactly one of train, validation or test, so the model is always judged on people it has never seen.

2 Embed slide and report

TITAN turns the slide into 768 numbers. An LLM cleans the report, then encodes it into 1024.

3 Late fusion

The two vectors are concatenated and passed through a small neural network with batch norm and dropout.

4 Pick one of 32

The network scores all 32 TCGA cancer types and returns the most likely one.

Food image classification, CNN from scratch

A ResNet-style network built from the ground up, no pretrained weights, to tell 80 dishes apart. Five residual blocks with skip connections and squeeze-and-excitation attention, trained with heavy augmentation, a warmup-then-cosine learning-rate schedule and weighted sampling for the imbalanced classes.

For the final submission I ensembled three models trained with different seeds and averaged 20 test-time augmentations per image.

63%test accuracy, single model (random guessing: ~1%)
~27kimages, 80 dishes
3 × 20models × TTA passes
